<Chapter n="01" phase="Overview & starting point" title="Launching UMA Plus—without a platform to launch it on">
<Figure src={product} caption="UMA Plus, one digital experience for many of Cornelsen's Schoolbook programmes - offering eBooks, additional teach and learn materials, lesson planner and more." alt="Covers of Cornelsen schoolbook programmes such as Deutschbuch, Context, Jo-Jo, Parallelo, À plus! and Universum, each labelled 'UMA Plus', next to a browser window showing the UMA lesson planner with a digital schoolbook page." />

<Columns>

In 2020, Cornelsen prepared to launch UMA Plus—the next generation of its most important digital teaching and learning product. Built around Cornelsen's schoolbooks, it combined digital editions for teachers and students with additional materials and tools for planning, presenting and delivering lessons.

UMA Plus was designed to support a wide range of subjects and many of Cornelsen's best-known schoolbook programmes within one digital experience.

</Columns>

<h3 class="t-display-h2 chapter__sub">Introducing the E-Bibliothek, a new place to launch digital products</h3>

<Figure src={eBibliothek} caption="From old platform to interim launchpad to future teach-and-learn platform" alt="Browser screenshots connected by arrows: Scook, Cornelsen's outdated teach and learn platform, sunset in November 2020; the UMA Plus experience planned to go live in September 2020; the E-Bibliothek on Cornelsen.de as interim home for all digital products; and lernen.cornelsen.de, the future platform, going live at Didacta 2021." />

<Columns>

Didacta, Germany's leading education fair, set a fixed deadline to introduce UMA Plus before purchasing decisions for the next school year. But the product would not be ready in time, while Cornelsen's existing learning platform, Scook, was being retired and its successor was still in development. Product leadership therefore decided to use the E-Bibliothek inside Cornelsen.de as the temporary launchpad for UMA Plus, eBooks and interactive lessons—the most cost-effective option available. My job was to make this interim journey work for customers.

This transition created a fragmented journey across marketing, eCommerce, licensing and classroom use, with funnel data already showing significant customer drop-offs. To create a shared view of these barriers and align teams around the experience, **I initiated a customer-journey programme covering the complete path** into Cornelsen's digital education ecosystem, and to support sales teams in showcasing and licensing the new product during the COVID-19 lockdowns and the cancellation of the in-person Didacta in 2020 and 2021.

</Columns>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="02" phase="Understand · The legacy journey" title="Two overlapping journeys had evolved around one product">
<Figure src={longJourney} caption="The interim Cornelsen Journey and the existing Scook Journey, screen by screen" alt="Screen-flow map in two rows. Top: the interim Cornelsen journey from homepage and UMA campaign via checkout and product activation to the E-Bibliothek and the new product. Bottom: the existing Scook journey, partly crossed out, with its own check-out and activation steps on the old platform." />

<Columns>

Mapping every screen and handover revealed two overlapping journeys: the established route through Scook and the emerging route through Cornelsen.de and its E-Bibliothek.

What appeared to be one product journey crossed marketing pages, product catalog structures, accounts, checkout systems, licence activation and two platforms. Making the entire experience visible gave teams a shared first view of its scale—and provided the basis for a more focused analysis.

</Columns>

<h3 class="t-display-h2 chapter__sub">The journey is organised in phases of varying user and business needs</h3>

<Figure src={phases} caption="Six journey phases - each revealing different needs for customer and business" alt="The ACCRA model – awareness, consideration, conversion, retention, advocacy – mapped onto two journey bars. Cornelsen.de: discover, evaluate, authenticate, purchase, activate, use. Scook.de: discover, use for free, activate, use, with a 'journey break' in between. Speech bubbles name the top job per phase: explore relevant digital materials, understand the value and choose the right product, verify teacher eligibility, complete checkout without losing context, purchase and connect the licence, enter and return to daily teaching and learning." />

<Columns>

To make the complexity actionable, I reorganised the journey around what customers and Cornelsen's eCommerce business were trying to achieve in each journey phase—from discovering the UMA product to using it in the classroom on a daily basis.

Applying the ACCRA journey model, a standard structure to organise user needs and business objectives, Cornelsen's specific journey phases started to become visible. These phases became the foundation of the next steps in the process to assess how well the experience actually worked.

</Columns>
</Chapter>

Together with Useeds and stakeholders across the organisation, we consolidated customer feedback, research and operational knowledge into one end-to-end view. This view - Cornelsen's digital customer journey - was full of problem zones… many more than we had originally anticipated.

</Columns>

<Figure src={keyMoments} caption="From findings to focus: clusters of evidence showed where to start, what came next and what came after" alt="Journey map with circles sized by the number of issues found, rated with sad, neutral or happy faces. <Columns>

Clusters of research evidence revealed where the journey created critical friction and where existing experiences already worked well—and informed what to tackle first, next and later.

The most significant barriers appeared at the handovers between journey phases: from marketing to product information, from evaluation to authentication, from purchase to activation and from eCommerce to classroom use.

To start unblocking the journey, we agreed with our colleagues from Marketing and the eCommerce product team, to dive deeper into the UMA Plus campaign page and make this our first priority for improvement ideation, integrated into the step-by-step release planning of the brand new UMA Plus.

</Columns>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="04" phase="Define · The release plan" title="From journey map to shared release plan">
<Figure src={management2} caption="The journey became the delivery structure: journey phases, cross-functional tasks and incremental releases managed in one shared roadmap tool" alt="Key moments from the journey map – schoolbook campaign pages, UMA campaign page, registration and customer number, demo use – connected by coloured arrows to the matching columns of a StoriesOnBoard board titled 'UMA 2.0 2020 Roadmap', organised by journey phase with release rows for March to June 2020." />

<Columns>

Once the key moments and dependencies were understood, I transferred the identified work into StoriesOnBoard. Its story-map structure allowed us to organise tasks by journey phase and release while connecting the work with Jira and Confluence. This was important because each release required coordinated changes across multiple touchpoints—from campaign pages and product catalogues to authentication, checkout and licence activation.

In bi-weekly release meetings with stakeholders, I could expand each release, walk through every journey moment and agree who needed to clarify, improve or contribute what.

</Columns>


<Figure src={management} caption="Expanded release view: work packages for the UMA Plus campaign page ahead of the demo release" alt="Expanded StoriesOnBoard release with story cards per journey phase. A highlighted column groups the work packages for the campaign page promoting the UMA Plus demo and beta release at Didacta." />

<Columns>

This shared roadmap made these dependencies manageable and accessible to stakeholders beyond the product and engineering teams. Most importantly, it preserved the end-to-end customer journey overview, while individual teams had transparency about their respective contributions.

</Columns>

<Chapter n="05" phase="Define · The value proposition" title="Reframing the UMA Plus value proposition narrative">
<Columns count={1}>

The UMA campaign page emerged as one of the journey's first critical key moments. Analytics showed that many visitors left without continuing towards purchase.

</Columns>

<FigureSplit src={benchmark} imageSide="right" title="Learning from digital product storytelling" caption="How digital services communicate value: Core value-, USP- and benefits copy, headlines, product visuals, calls-to-action and customer voices orchestrated in a single page scroll." alt="Annotated landing pages of Slack, Vimeo, InVision and Zoom. The Slack page is enlarged and broken down into core value, USPs, client voices and conversion trigger.">

Together with Marketing, Sales and Product Management, I reviewed the experience and identified two connected problems: the communication followed conventions developed for printed products, while the available page templates and UI components made it difficult to explain the value of a digital service.

In workshops, we analysed how services such as Slack, Zoom, InVision and Vimeo structured their stories around customer benefits, product evidence, social proof and conversion triggers. Their continuously evolving pages also demonstrated that value propositions should be tested and refined—not treated as static campaign assets.

</FigureSplit>

<FigureSplit src={analysis} imageSide="left" title="Connecting the proposition to user needs" caption="The Value Proposition Canvas for teachers, held against the existing campaign pages" alt="A filled-in Value Proposition Canvas for UMA Plus and the customer segment 'teacher', with sticky notes for gains, pains and jobs. Behind it, the existing landing page and demo sub-page, annotated with questions such as 'very technical copywriting' and 'what is UMA in a nutshell?'.">

Benchmarking showed us how digital services communicated value, but not which message would resonate with Cornelsen's customers.

Using Strategyzer's Value Proposition Canvas, I facilitated the collaborative mapping of needs, pains and desired gains of teachers, students and parents. In the workshop with Marketing we then assessed Cornelsen's existing copy, visuals and storylines against those user motivations and drivers.

This exposed where the campaign described product features without connecting them clearly to classroom challenges or meaningful customer benefits. The analysis gave Marketing, Product and UX a shared foundation for reframing the UMA Plus story around the value it created for its users.

</FigureSplit>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="06" phase="Design · The campaign page" title="From static campaign page to evolving product story">
<Figure src={campaign} caption="From campaign asset to evolving product story: content, UI improvements and user feedback combined across releases." alt="Three versions of the UMA campaign landing page side by side – before (2019), after (Q1 2020) and later (Q2 2020), connected by 'user feedback' arrows – annotated with remaining barriers in red and improvements in blue, such as clearer product introduction, better activation guidance, a more prominent trailer and a shorter page." />

<Columns>

With the learnings made in the workshops, step-by-step a new way of working was established to improve the campaign page release after release.

Together, we refined the campaign templates, redesigned supporting UI components and developed new editorial content. Successive iterations introduced clearer product benefits, stronger calls to action, better activation guidance, better positioning of product demo trailer and a shorter, more focused page structure. User feedback helped us review and refine these changes throughout the UMA Plus rollouts in 2020 and 2021.


Beyond improving one campaign page, the collaboration established a new way of working: Combining value-proposition design, editorial and UI iterations together with user feedback within one iterative release process. This approach could then support other digital Cornelsen products and the continuing transition from Scook to lernen.cornelsen.de.

[Check out the latest version of the UMA campaign page](https://www.cornelsen.de/digital/unterrichtsmanager). The team has continued to evolve it since.

</Columns>
</Chapter>
