<Columns>

JOYN.ME and the underlying SevenOne interaction system, was an [award-winning](https://ifdesign.com/de/winner-ranking/project/sevenone-interaction-ecosystem/564461) TV companion experience that allowed audiences to vote, play quizzes and participate in live prime-time shows of the Pro7Sat1 broadcasting group. Following JOYN's acquisition by Pro7Sat1, these capabilities had to be integrated into the JOYN mobile apps and the separate JOYN.ME service sunset.

The deadline was fixed by broadcasting schedules and operational dependencies, but the integration strategy was not. Two teams entered the project with different goals, product cultures and expectations towards the future of interactive TV Show experiences within JOYN.

</Columns>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="02" phase="Understand · The context" title="Three competing perspectives shaped a bumpy start">
<Figure src={tensions} caption="Three tensions between JOYN and JOYN.ME - resolved by defining a shared, feasible MVP" alt="Three pairs of opposing goals, labelled definition of value, product ambition and delivery reality: JOYN's streaming adoption versus JOYN.ME's fan engagement, a lightweight capability versus a full fan world ecosystem, and fixed broadcast commitments versus an uncertain technical integration. All three lead to 'Define a shared, feasible MVP'." />

<Columns count={3}>

The integration began with three unresolved tensions:

- **Business value** — JOYN prioritised app adoption, onboarding and watch time, while JOYN.ME measured success through deeper engagement with individual shows. Interactivity mattered strongly to some fans, but was not a primary reason most people used JOYN.
- **Product ambition** — JOYN aimed for a lightweight participation capability that felt coherent within its streaming experience. JOYN.ME envisioned a prominent fan world eco system that connected audiences with show narratives before, during and after broadcast.
- **Delivery reality** — JOYN.ME was due to sunset in October 2023, while commitments to upcoming TV productions had already been made. At the same time, the feasibility of integrating Seven.One's interaction ecosystem into JOYN's React Native architecture remained uncertain.

</Columns>

<Columns count={1}>

Resolving these tensions and finding consensus in [a feasible, shared MVP](#chapter-05) became the project's first task. But before, we all needed to better understand [Interactive TV Shows](#chapter-03), [the Fan](#chapter-03) and her/his [journey into participation](#chapter-04).

</Columns>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="03" phase="Understand · Interactive TV shows" title="Participation could expand what streaming meant to audiences">
<Figure src={extendedProp} caption="Interactive TV shows - unifying lean-back and lean-forward experiences in one entertainment format" alt="Diagram of an interactive TV show at the centre, surrounded by lean-back streaming media – live stream, episode stream, clips and trailers, show details and news – and lean-forward activities from gaming and social media – live voting, live quizzes, surveys and polls, chat." />

<Columns>

Streaming TV Shows with JOYN was primarily designed for lean-back streaming: finding a programme and watching it.

JOYN.ME introduced a different behaviour—leaning forward to vote, predict, answer or respond while a TV show was live was another way to interact with streaming content, it was adding engagement, a KPI we didn't measure and designed for yet.

Our job was to explore whether participation in interactive TV Shows could add a new dimension to JOYN's value proposition without disrupting its core streaming experience.

**We, at the time, just didn't know how well participation would be anticipated, liked and fancied by JOYN's lean back user base.**

</Columns>

<h3 class="t-display-h2 chapter__sub">Could fan engagement activate more lean back entertainment?</h3>

<Figure src={insights} caption="Interactive TV experiences had not been a priority for most of JOYNs regular user base, but data still showed strong engagement during selected live shows at primetime" alt="Friends on a sofa vote with their phones during a live TV show. An overlay summarises a MaxDiff study with 1,600+ JOYN users (February 2023): content choice and live-TV access ranked highest, interactive entertainment lower but relevant to specific audiences. Conclusion: high relevance in specific moments, but not for every viewer." />

<Columns>

JOYN.ME usage data showed that participation in JOYN.ME could generate strong engagement of up to 300,000 users participating during selected shows at primetime. However, existing research offered little insight into audience motivations or how participation fitted into JOYN's broader streaming experience.

I initiated a MaxDiff study conducted by our user research team with more than 1,600 JOYN users ranking interactive TV entertainment way below established streaming needs such as content choice, live-TV access or offline consumption. As a result, we needed to treat the interactivity of TV Shows as a high-potential experience for specific live audiences—but not as a universal priority for every JOYN user.

This insight affected the design of the MVP onboarding experience and how deep, how visible and how permanent interactive TV shows became available in JOYN's mobile experience architecture.

</Columns>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="04" phase="Understand · The user journey" title="Users had seconds to move from broadcast to interaction">
<Figure src={journey} layout="content" caption="Step-by-step journeying into interactive TV Show participation" alt="Journey diagram: a TV moderator asks 'Who has to leave? Vote with JOYN on your phone today!' with a QR code. A timeline shows: before the show – notify me and check out tonight's candidates; on start – watch live and get ready to vote; during the show – vote now on the second screen and chat; after the show – follow-up interactions and catch-up." />

For most users, participation started on the TV, not in the app. A moderator or on-screen prompt invited viewers to act while the programme continued live. New users then had 30 seconds up to 2 minutes to scan a code, find or install JOYN, complete onboarding and reach the active interaction before the moment passed.

Mapping this cross-device journey exposed the central experience requirement: **every avoidable step reduced the chance of successful participation and users needed to be guided instantly into participation.**
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="05" phase="Define · The MVP" title="Four user goals aligned ambition and MVP delivery">
<Figure src={levels} caption="Four levels of integration - from shallow to deep - from required to vision - from 01 to 04" alt="Four columns from shallow to deep integration with phone wireframes: 01 'One tap away' (attention), 02 'One central home' (discovery), both marked 'required for launch'; 03 'Connected to the show' (details context), marked 'next up after launch'; 04 'Participate while streaming' (participation in the live player), marked 'longer-term vision'." />

<Columns>

I framed four progressive levels of integration around the user goal each one served:

1. **One tap away** — Use contextual notifications and prominent teasers to make time-sensitive participation quick to enter and difficult to miss.
2. **One central home** — Provide a reliable destination for discovering and returning to interactive shows before, during and after broadcast.
3. **Connected to the show** — Embed participation within the existing programme-/format-page and its wider JOYN journey.
4. **Participate while streaming** — Bring interaction into the live player and EPG experience, removing additional steps between watching and participating.

The first two levels defined the MVP: a reliable experience that could meet the deadline while allowing us to learn before investing more deeply in interactive TV Show capabilities. This required trade-offs. JOYN postponed integration into programme-/format-pages and the live player, while JOYN.ME accepted no primary navigation entry until demand was proven. Instead, a permanent lane within "Neu & Beliebt" gave fans permanent visible access to interactive shows.

</Columns>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="06" phase="Design · Required for launch" title="1 MVP = 2 Goals: “One tap away” and “One central home”">

<h3 class="t-display-h2 chapter__sub">Goal 1: Timely guidance into interactive TV Show participation - “One tap away”</h3>

<Figure src={level1} caption="One-tap-away - a fast entry into single tv interactions by notifications and teasers" alt="Phone wireframes from timely access to permanent access: app notification 'Voting started! The Masked Singer' on the lock screen, in-app hero notification, hero lane live teaser and TV show interaction lane on the JOYN home, all leading to the voting dialog 'Vote now! Select who is out' with four candidates. Marked 'required for launch'." />

<Columns count={1}>

As there was only a very short period of time available for users to act upon a moderator's prompt on TV, we needed to make sure an invitation to participate was not to be missed out on the smartphone.

By the use of notifications and various teaser formats, triggered from within SevenOnes interactive eco system studio software, we delivered a moderator's prompt into the hands and attention of a JOYN user in real time, enabling instant participation on "one tap away".

</Columns>

<h3 class="t-display-h2 chapter__sub">Goal 2: Establish “One central home” for all interactive TV shows</h3>

<Figure src={level2} caption="One central home - guiding users into a central home for all interactive TV shows on air" alt="Phone wireframes: interactive TV lane on the JOYN home, a new in-app hero teaser, the 'Jetzt mitmachen!' home listing live shows, a show home with access to all interactions, and the voting dialog. Marked 'required for launch'." />

<Columns count={1}>

To keep the comfort established by the JOYN.ME app in providing access to all currently broadcasted interactive TV Shows, a dedicated and prominently placed “Mitmachen!”-Lane on the JOYN Home screen was introduced, calling a landing page to browse all interactive Shows currently on air and with all interactive quizzes, votings and sweepstakes made available by the editorial team.

Additionally, further teasers into all or specific TV Show interactions could be placed inside the new hero-teaser lane in case a user has disabled app notifications.

</Columns>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="07" phase="Deliver · The visual system" title="Participation needed to feel like JOYN">
<Figure src={uiStructure} caption="Two layers: the JOYN system frames each interaction, the show layer carries its brand" alt="'One product system for many show identities': annotated phone screens. Hero teaser, pop-up notification and modal dialog come from the JOYN system; images, text, brand elements, interaction teaser cards, list components and confirmation animations are delivered by the show layer via the DAVE API." />

<Columns>

JOYN.ME's visual language had been designed as a standalone experience. Inside JOYN, interactive content needed to remain expressive enough for individual show brands while still feeling coherent with the product's navigation, components and interaction patterns.

Finally, the designers of both teams aligned and shipped a flexible visual system that combined JOYN conventions with format-specific colours, imagery and game mechanics. In essence we built a little design system dedicated to support interactive TV shows.

</Columns>

<h3 class="t-display-h2 chapter__sub">Deadline met, interactions shipped, but it was not done yet.</h3>

<Figure src={shipped} caption="Five interactive TV shows, enabled by the December 2023 delivery and aired from late 2023 to early 2024" alt="Overview of five shows delivered in time. A fully integrated live-player experience was not technically feasible within the deadline. We therefore prioritised fast entry points, a central "Mitmachen" destination and the interaction flows required by the scheduled productions.

This allowed five prime-time shows—including *Wer stiehlt mir die Show*, *Promi Big Brother* and *The Masked Singer*—to launch with interactive companion experiences while JOYN.ME was getting retired without losing its fan base.

However, we discovered another challenge: some of the interactive content seemed to lack quality again and again.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="08" phase="Learn & scale · Editorial quality" title="Better tools helped editorial teams to create better interactions content">
More and more interactive content was produced, but the editorial teams struggled to produce consistent quality: copy text overflowed, copy or images went missing, color schemes seemed broken.

Because contributors had different levels of interactive-design experience, we created guidelines and reusable templates covering visual quality, interaction patterns and production best practices. This helped editorial teams to quicker onboard new editors, to create more consistent content and reduced the need for support from the designer of my team to zero.
