<Chapter n="01" phase="Overview & Starting point" title="A legal deadline—and nobody accountable for meeting it">

<Figure src={context} caption="The challenge framed in three buckets - deadline, 5 platforms and operations gap" alt="Three panels: 01 Legal deadline – EAA, June 2025. 02 Five platforms – web, iOS, Android, OTT and Fire TV, one cross-platform experience for live and on-demand streaming. 03 Operating gap – no shared owner, no shared roadmap and priorities." />

In 2024, JOYN faced a fixed deadline: meet the requirements of the European Accessibility Act by June 2025. Yet no team owned accessibility across its web, mobile and TV products. Leadership understood the urgency but feared that compliance could derail existing roadmaps or require platforms to be rebuilt.

I stepped in to establish a baseline, make the challenge actionable and create the organisational ownership needed to move forward. Accessibility wasn't only a compliance requirement. If JOYN wanted to position itself as the DACH “superstreamer,” the experience needed to work for a broader range of audiences.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="02" phase="Understand · A11y compliance" title="First we had to make JOYN’s accessibility tangible">

<Figure src={audits} caption="A roadmap to understanding - a11y audits planned for 2024" alt="Timeline of WCAG audits: web and mobile in Q3 2024 with Nexum, TV in Q4 2024 with Railslove, and PIKSL user interviews across all platforms in December 2024." />

Together with my head of UX research and three specialised partners (Nexum, Railslove and PIKSL), we established JOYN’s first accessibility baseline. Technical audits assessed representative journeys across web, iOS, Android, Fire TV and other TV platforms.

In parallel, interviews with people with disabilities and watching a great variety of YouTube clips of impaired users talking about their ways of using digital services helped us gain first understanding about how those barriers affected real streaming experiences.

**Combining technical evidence with lived experience prevented accessibility from becoming a purely legal checklist.**
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="03" phase="Understand · Audit tactics" title="Then we needed to decide which moments to analyse">

<Figure src={journey} caption="User journey touchpoints in scope for the a11y audit" alt="19 representative touchpoints grouped into Legal, Discover, Business and Streaming – from CMP dialog, home and search to sign-up, age verification, player and legal pages." />

<Columns>

Picking a representative journey allowed us to audit the moments that mattered most to users, the business and legal compliance. The journey illustrates a typical walkthrough of a user coming to JOYN. From discovering something to stream by browsing or searching, hitting the reg-wall, youth-verification and finally entering streaming of live- or on-demand content.

Rather than auditing every screen, I proposed a set of unique templates and aligned them with internal stakeholders and our audit partners, Nexum and Railslove. The resulting 19 touchpoints focused the audit on critical user, business and compliance moments while revealing systemic issues across platforms.

</Columns>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="04" phase="Understand · User needs" title="Then we learned accessibility is not one user need">

<Figure src={empathy} caption="Empathy persona maps for workshops with stakeholders" alt="Three persona map workshop boards – visual, hearing and cognitive impairments – with notes, video stills and user quotes." />

User interviews and WCAG analysis revealed four groups of barriers: **visual, auditory, cognitive and motor**. To make these needs tangible and support regular feedback in our agile work, we translated them into video-supported persona maps focused on behaviours, assistive technologies and content needs—not demographics.

This gave product, engineering and editorial teams a shared language for discussing accessibility beyond compliance.

<Chapter n="05" phase="Understand · A11y tech" title="Assistive technology revealed an invisible layer of UX">

<Figure src={assistive} caption="Screenreader interaction visualised - listening to the interface" alt="Four steps of a screen reader interaction: focus reaches a content tile, the interface exposes label and status, the screen reader announces it, the user decides what to do next." />

<Columns>

Watching people use screen readers and voice control revealed **an often-invisible layer of UX**. Beyond visual hierarchy, meaningful labels, useful descriptions and logical focus order were essential to navigation and orientation.

**Supporting assistive technologies became our highest priority**, shaping team roadmaps, definitions of done and how designers and engineers designed, reviewed and shipped UI components.

</Columns>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="06" phase="Define · The challenge" title="The audits revealed 4 systemic challenges">

<Figure src={issues} caption="Audit results - A11y compliance by platform and overarching issue themes" alt="Audit findings per platform: Web 53 (critical), OTT 18 (serious), ATV 51, Android 38 and iOS 42 (moderate). Four recurring systemic barriers: navigation and orientation, content identification, player operation, and forms, account and feedback." />

The audits identified an overwhelming amount of issues, but a list of defects was not yet a strategy. Some barriers were specific to individual platforms, while others—such as missing labels, inconsistent focus behaviour and insufficient content metadata—reappeared across the ecosystem.

We needed to distinguish urgent platform fixes from shared structural, systemic issues that required coordinated investment aka becoming candidates for shared and coordinated roadmap priorities.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="07" phase="Define · A11y ops" title="A cross-functional task force enabled shared learning">

<Figure src={ops} caption="A11y taskforce set up and operational landscape to make accessibility compliance a sustainable practice" alt="Diagram of the A11y task force for accessibility coordination and ops at the centre, connected through shared learning to five product teams, editorial and curation, legal and compliance, and external A11y specialists." />

<Columns>

As the work outgrew our small group, I used audit and research findings to secure support from Sustainability and Inclusion, our CPO and the CTO office. This enabled me to assemble a dedicated task force spanning product management, engineering, UX and delivery leadership.

With Nexum and Railslove, we outlined coordinated accessibility priorities for roadmap planning and established a Confluence knowledge base, while helping product teams resolve platform-specific issues independently.

</Columns>
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="08" phase="Define · A11y roadmap" title="A shared roadmap turned findings into action">

<Figure src={roadmap} caption="A two-layer approach to manage time critical and continuous a11y issue resolution across 5 product teams and other stakeholders" alt="Two-layer roadmap: layer 1 legal and editorial priorities, layer 2 six priorities for continuous issue resolution across web, iOS, Android, OTT and ATV." />

To prevent teams from becoming overwhelmed, we translated the audit findings into a two-layer roadmap and provided structured guidance in Confluence about how to resolve the issue at hand.

Product teams prioritised fixes within their platforms, while the cross-functional accessibility programme addressed shared requirements across product design, content publishing, legal reporting and assistive-technology support.

**This gave teams autonomy without losing a common direction.**
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="09" phase="Ideate and deliver · A11y user journey" title="Designing pathways into accessible content">

<Figure src={pathways} caption="Illustrated pathways into accessible streaming" alt="End-to-end journey from find to trust with four discovery pathways – brand, channel, format and direct search – leading into accessible streaming, including a new A11y hub." />

The audits showed that accessibility also depended on **content discovery**. Our research identified four entry patterns and their key touchpoints, revealing a gap: a dedicated **“Barrierefrei” hub** for accessible content.

The hub would improve SEO discoverability and give accessible content a new home as legacy ProSiebenSat.1 channel sites were retired in favour of JOYN’s channel pages and publishing platform.
</Chapter>

<Chapter n="10" phase="Deliver · A11y content hub" title="Accessible content also had to become discoverable">

<Figure src={hub} caption="A11y content hub “Barrierefreie-Inhalte” to enable curation of a11y enabled content" alt="The Barrierefrei hub shown on TV, web and phone, with the A11y content hub check-list 2026." />

Together with editorial and curation teams, we developed a **“Barrierefrei” hub across web, mobile and TV**, bringing audio-described, subtitled and signed content into one curated destination while broader accessibility improvements continued.

**Screen reader navigation, including VoiceOver,** made the hub easier to use, while SEO helped audiences discover its content. Accessible content, produced by SevenOne, has never been easier to discover than today.

<Chapter n="11" phase="Embed · Best practices" title="The deadline started the work and new routines made it last.">

<Figure src={lre} caption="A model for change to move from shared understanding to a new way of working" alt="Three steps: Learn (audit readouts, interviews with impaired users, deep dives, empathy workshops), Ritualise (bi-weekly A11y UX CoPs, monthly content check-ins, roadmap priorities, sprint planning), Embed (Definition of Done, release audits in QA, Figma plug-ins and checklist, design system check-marks). Conclusion: accessibility awareness became part of how JOYN works." />

A recurring pattern emerged: learn, ritualise, embed. Audits and research created knowledge. Shared priorities moved accessibility into roadmaps. Communities of practice, design-system standards and editorial routines then helped make that knowledge repeatable.

Accessibility was no longer treated only as a deadline-driven project; it began to become part of how JOYN designed, developed and published streaming experiences.
</Chapter>
